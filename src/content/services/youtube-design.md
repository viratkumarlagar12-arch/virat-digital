## Designed for the size people actually see

YouTube shows the same thumbnail at very different sizes: large on a TV, medium on a laptop, and tiny in the sidebar and in mobile search. Most people browse on phones, so a thumbnail has to work at the smallest size first. That means one clear subject, a few words at most, strong contrast, and nothing important where YouTube puts the video's duration.

I run my own channel, [@viratdigitalmarketing](https://youtube.com/@viratdigitalmarketing), so I design with YouTube's crops, overlays and browsing habits in mind.

## A look viewers recognise

A channel is easier to remember when its thumbnails share a system: the same type, a consistent colour, a recognisable layout for each kind of video. I design that system first, then each thumbnail inside it, so your uploads look related without looking identical.

## Banners that survive every crop

A channel banner is one image cropped very differently on a TV, a desktop and a phone. Only the centre safe area is visible everywhere, so your name and the one thing you want people to know go there, and the rest is designed to crop gracefully.
