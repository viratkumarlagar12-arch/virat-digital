## Start with the work, not the tool

The most useful automations are usually unglamorous: the enquiry that gets copied from a form into a spreadsheet and then into a WhatsApp reply, the weekly report rebuilt by hand, the same social caption rewritten for three platforms. I start by mapping how a task is done today and how long it takes, so we automate what actually costs you time.

## AI where it helps, people where it matters

AI models are good at drafting, summarising, sorting and extracting. They're not good at being responsible for the result. So the workflows I build keep a person in the loop wherever a mistake would matter: drafts arrive for approval, summaries link back to the source, and anything unusual is flagged for you to check.

## Small, reliable steps

Rather than one large system, I build small automations that each do one job and can be tested on their own. They're easier to trust, easier to change when your business changes, and easy to switch off if you ever need to.
