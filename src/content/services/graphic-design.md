## What a brand has to do

A small business usually meets its customers in many small places at once: a signboard, a WhatsApp profile picture, an invoice, a delivery box, an Instagram post. A brand identity is what makes all of those look like the same business. When it's missing, every new graphic gets made from scratch and the business looks a little different everywhere it appears.

## How I approach it

Every identity starts with questions rather than sketches: who you sell to, who you compete with, and where your logo will actually be seen. A logo that only works large on a white background fails on a phone screen or a rubber stamp, so I test each direction at real sizes and in one colour before refining it.

From the chosen direction I build the parts you'll reuse every day: colours that hold up in print and on screen, typefaces you can actually install, and a short guide that explains the rules in plain language. The aim is that you, or anyone you hire later, can make a new poster or post that still looks like you.

## Social media graphics

Social graphics work best as a system: a few templates for the kinds of posts you make most often, built on your brand colours and type. You get finished posts, plus templates you can reuse in Canva or Photoshop.
